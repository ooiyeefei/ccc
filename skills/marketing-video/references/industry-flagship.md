# Industry flagship - a month in the life of one business

Industry is a parameter, not a family. Swapping the industry (a cattle farm, a retail shop, a clinic) changes the brief and the staged workspace, never the process. The flagship is a marketing film in its long shape: the viewer is a stranger from that trade asking "is this how my business would run?", and it ends on one ask.

## The industry brief

Fill it before the storyboard: `film-line/references/industry-brief.md`. It is the film's input; the marketing brief (`SKILL.md`, step 1) is the film's contract, and one file may hold both, shown with the storyboard for one nod.

## The shape

Open > chapters in the order the owner lives the month > the books tie out (ledger and reports traced back to the paper) > the hand-off to the accountant > one ask.

- **Opening.** The first thing heard is the month's pain, in two sentences, in the trade's words. It may sit over a prop, a plain card or a real screen that shows the pain. The product moves from the next beat, and the title lands after it. A rendered opening is an approved exception recorded in the storyboard, never a substitute for a chapter.
- **Chapters.** Each opens with a silent chapter card (`demo-video/references/structure.md`) and is one job in the trade: a pain, one proof moment, what it means. The "two or three values" rule is for short films; a flagship carries one proof moment per chapter, and the chapters may be grouped under three values in the brief.
- Order chapters by the owner's month: money in and out first, people next, questions to the assistant late, the accountant last.
- A person decides every approval and presses every post and payment on camera. The film never says the product pays anyone.
- **The plan names what else the takes must serve**: the 60 to 90 second films per domain and the tutorial tasks (see "One recording, three uses" below). Each beat then carries a step id and proof text, so a later cut finds it.
- A recap card is optional. The default is to close on the one ask with no recap and no thanks card.
- Length follows the chapters. Eight to twelve minutes is the target; if the chapters give seven, the film is seven and the report names the levers (an extra chapter that has been rehearsed live), never padding. A flagship's voice is near-continuous: the shipped farm film ran 747 seconds with 1,736 spoken words, 2.3 words per second over the whole film, so words divided by 2.3 gives the length. Its twelve chapters ran from 18 to 161 seconds; a chapter of 11 to 16 lines takes two to three minutes and one of 3 to 5 lines takes 30 to 55 seconds.
- Record order follows state. A frame that needs the data before a later chapter changes it (three bills still unpaid) is recorded first.

## One recording, three uses

Record the flagship with tutorial-grade coverage, as the plan states: every beat is a step with proof text and a cue. The same take bank then yields:

1. the flagship, cut for story;
2. a 60 to 90 second marketing film per domain, each with its own storyboard and its own voice written to its own cut;
3. signed-in tutorials per task (`help-center-tutorial`), each from its own step sheet, using the take only where its steps match.

Mark in the sidecar which cue belongs to which step so a later cut finds it. Cut lines for one use never leak into another: marketing voice is story-led, tutorial text is imperative.

## Delivery

Chapters for YouTube, a subtitle file, a 16:9 cut if a band is burned in, and a faster-playback variant beside the normal one if the house rules ask for it (`film-line/references/house-rules-groot.md`).
