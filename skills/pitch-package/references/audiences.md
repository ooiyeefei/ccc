# Audiences - three lenses, one product

Do not rebuild assets per audience. Swap the hook, the emphasis beats, and the close; the deck body, demo film, and architecture story stay shared.

| | Hackathon judges | VC / investors | Customer |
|---|---|---|---|
| They silently ask | "Is it real? Is it yours? Does it fit the track?" | "Is the market big, why now, what compounds?" | "Does this fix MY pain, and what does it take to run here?" |
| Lead with | The proof beat (the thing working, live or on film) | The problem's size and the why-now | Their pain, in their words and numbers |
| The demo is | Evidence of "real and yours" | Evidence the moat exists | A mirror: data shaped like theirs |
| Close with | Track fit + the throughline | The compounding loop + the ask | A concrete pilot ask |

## Hackathon judges

- **Proof over polish.** The strongest beat is the product doing the thing live or verifiably on film (e.g. a learning loop closing on camera with a similarity score that is not 1.0 - visible evidence it isn't staged). Name the evidence in the narration.
- **Rubric mapping.** Read the track's own wording and answer its test explicitly. A sponsor-track bar like "the platform must be core, not just hosting" deserves a literal argument: "remove X and the product stops working" for each core claim.
- **Honesty discipline.** Judges probe. Anything not wired is roadmap, framed as roadmap. Generic model tiers when on-screen labels differ. One caught overclaim and every true claim is doubted - this is the highest-stakes audience for the claim-only-what's-wired rule.
- Team intro stays short; deep tech lives in appendix slides for Q&A.

## VC / investors

- Lead with the problem's scale and the why-now (regulatory wave, licensing wave, platform shift) before any product pixel.
- **The moat is what compounds.** Frame the differentiator as a loop that gets stronger with use ("every decision an analyst makes becomes precedent the next case cites") rather than a feature list.
- The throughline doubles as the investable one-liner; it should survive being repeated by the VC to their partners without you in the room.
- The team earns more airtime here than anywhere else - real credentials, one line each.
- End with the ask (raise, intros, pilot partners). A pitch without an ask is a documentary.

## Customer

- Open with THEIR pain in THEIR vocabulary and volumes ("your analysts clear a thousand alerts a day at twenty minutes each"), not your category jargon.
- Demo on data shaped like theirs; a generic demo reads as someone else's product.
- ROI math conservative and explicit - one under-claimed number beats three impressive ones.
- Anticipate the operational questions in appendix: security posture, deployment model, what integration actually takes.
- Close with a small, concrete pilot ask ("what would it take to run this against one week of your alerts?") - never "any questions?".
