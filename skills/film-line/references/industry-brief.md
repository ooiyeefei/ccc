# The industry brief

Industry (a cattle farm, a retail shop, a clinic) is a parameter of a film, never a family. It is filled in once, before the plan, in this one brief, and every family reads the same one: the planner for the story, the stager for the business. It is not a staging reference, so a planner reads it without seeing the staging steps.

| Field | Staging needs it | What goes in |
|---|---|---|
| Business | yes | a fictitious company: plain made-up name, what it buys and sells, size, place |
| Cast | yes | roles (owner, manager, employee), never names in a voice |
| The month | yes | four to eight events every owner of this trade knows: buying, selling, paying people, month end |
| Words and units | yes | the trade's vocabulary (cattle by the head, a shop by the item); the voice uses them |
| Coverage | yes | what the product does for this trade and where it stops; this becomes the "does not show" list |
| Pains | no | three, in the trade's own words (`marketing-video`) |
| Honest scope | no | one sentence on what the film does not claim |
| Proof moments | no | one per chapter, each a real frame |

If coverage cannot be filled from what is known, take a read-only look at the product or ask the owner, and mark the field unverified meanwhile. A chapter with no product behind it is better discovered at the gate than at the smoke take.

Staging (`staging.md`) turns the first five fields into a business: the chart of accounts with the trade's own accounts on top of the standard ones, the items and parties in the trade's units, history dated in the past for as long as the plan's month needs (the plan's length wins over any default), and the cast as real logins. Tutorials are keyed by domain, so they reuse whichever industry workspace exists; do not stage a second business to re-film the same screen.
