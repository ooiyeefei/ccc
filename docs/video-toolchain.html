Which tool does which part of a video

One framework, three video types, and a small set of tools that plug in at different points. This is the map: what records the real product, what renders the animated scenes, and what glues them together.

The one idea

Every video is some mix of two kinds of footage, joined by a third tool. The video type decides the proportions.

Record the real product

A crisp screen recording of the actual app, driven like a person uses it. This is the proof. Xvfb + browser automation + ffmpeg.

Render the story

Animated scenes, mocked or idealised UI, charts, titles — built in code. This is the wrapper. Remotion.

Glue it together

Crossfade the clips into one film and lay narration over it. ffmpeg stitches; pitch-craft scripts.

The pipeline

Read top to bottom. The two footage lanes run independently, then converge at the stitch. Marketing films are heavy on the violet lane; tutorials and pitch demos live almost entirely in the teal one.

So where does Remotion come in?

Only when the scene is rendered rather than filmed — motion graphics, an idealised or mocked screen, an animated chart, a title card, a data reveal. That is the marketing / explainer genre. In a pitch demo or a tutorial there is nothing for Remotion to do: the whole job is the real product on screen, so it is all Lane 1.

The line that keeps it honest

Mocked and rendered scenes are legitimate in the wrapper; the one real capture is the proof. Never pass a Remotion-rendered UI off as a live recording. That is the same "show the real thing" ethic the demo-video contract enforces at the frame level, applied one level up at the film level.

The Groot Solo marketing film is the worked example: seven scenes, six rendered in Remotion (cold-open, statement, the chat, the e-invoice flow, the brief, the close) and one real capture of the live P&L page — all crossfaded with ffmpeg, on the real Geist font and logo.
